# Landing review: U16 (fresh reviewer, screenshots only)

Source: `docs/ux/review/r16/landing/` (desktop 1440×900, phone Pixel 7 at 412 px, and desktop with reduced motion for "jobs"). Persona: a hackathon judge with 3 minutes who has never heard of Disc, x402 or Remora. I judged only what the screenshots show. Contrast figures are estimates sampled from the JPEGs, so they are approximate.

## Five-second read

Remora is a product for AI agents ("remoras") that run errands and pay for data. Each one has a spending limit that Sui enforces, a memory stored on Walrus, and a receipt for every payment. The first thing to click is clear: the white "Try a Researcher · $1 limit · no wallet" button. "Watch the demo" is a clearly quieter second choice, and "Try it" in the nav repeats the main action. The money story runs well from section to section: "Paying is where it breaks" leads to the four moments, then "One limit. Many jobs.", then the demo, the proof and what's real. The hero headline ("Always-on agents you actually own.") is about ownership, so a judge only learns about the limit from the subtitle and the button. The new "jobs" section sits in the right place and makes the page feel real: ten dated jobs, each with a short receipt link. Its weak spot is that it never shows the limit its headline promises. It also states prices and a service count that disagree with other sections.
